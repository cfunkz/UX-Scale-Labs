When to use it

Complex, high-stakes or time-pressured tasks: dashboards, operational tools, medical and industrial interfaces.

How it is scored

  1. Each dimension is rated from 0 to 100 in steps of 5 (21 marks, as in the paper form).
  2. Raw TLX is the mean of the six ratings. Hart (2006) reviews evidence that it performs comparably to the weighted version.
  3. Note the Performance scale runs from Perfect (0) to Failure (100), so on every dimension lower means less workload.

Frequently asked questions about the NASA-TLX

How do I calculate the NASA-TLX score?

  1. Each dimension is rated from 0 to 100 in steps of 5 (21 marks, as in the paper form).
  2. Raw TLX is the mean of the six ratings. Hart (2006) reviews evidence that it performs comparably to the weighted version.
  3. Note the Performance scale runs from Perfect (0) to Failure (100), so on every dimension lower means less workload.

This calculator applies these steps automatically as soon as every item is answered.

What is a good NASA-TLX score?

Lower means less workload. There are no universal cut-offs, so compare designs or task variants against each other and look at which dimension drives the score.

How long does the NASA-TLX take?

The NASA-TLX has 6 items and takes about 2 minutes. Typical use: Complex, high-stakes or time-pressured tasks: dashboards, operational tools, medical and industrial interfaces.

Can I calculate NASA-TLX scores for many participants at once?

Yes. Collect the answers in Excel, Google Forms, Qualtrics or any other tool with one row per participant and columns Q1 to Q6, then upload the table as CSV or JSON on the "Score a dataset" tab. If participants filled in the NASA-TLX here and sent you their downloads, drop all the PDF, CSV, JSON or ZIP files in at once instead. You get a score for every person, plus the mean, standard deviation and 95% confidence interval.

What is similar to the NASA-TLX?
