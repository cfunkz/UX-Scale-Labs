Task completion rate calculator

Enter how many participants completed a task. Get the best estimate of the true completion rate, an adjusted-Wald confidence interval that works with small samples, and a comparison with a benchmark.

Free and private: everything runs in your browser and nothing you enter or upload is sent anywhere.

How it is calculated

  1. The confidence interval is the adjusted-Wald interval: add z²/2 successes and z²/2 failures, then use the usual Wald formula. It keeps close to the stated confidence even with very small samples (Agresti & Coull, 1998).
  2. The best point estimate follows Lewis and Sauro (2006): the Wilson estimate when the observed rate is 50% or less, the Laplace estimate ((x + 1) ÷ (n + 2)) when it is 90% or more, and the observed rate in between.
  3. The benchmark comparison checks whether the whole interval lies above or below the benchmark.

References

  1. Agresti, A., & Coull, B. A. (1998). Approximate is better than "exact" for interval estimation of binomial proportions. The American Statistician, 52(2), 119–126. doi.org/10.1080/00031305.1998.10480550
  2. Lewis, J. R., & Sauro, J. (2006). When 100% really isn't 100%: Improving the accuracy of small-sample estimates of completion rates. Journal of Usability Studies, 1(3), 136–150.
  3. Sauro, J., & Lewis, J. R. (2016). Quantifying the User Experience: Practical Statistics for User Research (2nd ed.). Morgan Kaufmann.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a task completion rate?

Divide the number of participants who completed the task by the number who attempted it. With small samples that raw percentage is only a rough guide, so report it with a confidence interval like the one calculated above.

Why not just report 100% if everyone succeeded?

With a small sample, 10 out of 10 does not mean every future user will succeed. The adjusted-Wald interval for 10/10 runs from about 68% to 100%, and the Laplace best estimate is about 92%, which is a more honest summary.

What is a good task completion rate?

Across many usability studies the average completion rate is about 78%. Rates well below that usually point to a serious problem with the task, although critical tasks such as checkout or sign-up should be held to a higher standard.
