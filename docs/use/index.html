Frequently asked questions about the USE

How do I calculate the USE score?

  1. Each subscale is the mean of its items (1–7, higher is better); the overall score is the mean of all 30 items.
  2. Widely used, but its psychometric properties were never fully published, so treat comparisons with care.

This calculator applies these steps automatically as soon as every item is answered.

What is a good USE score?

Higher is better on a 1–7 scale. There are no published norms, so compare subscales and product versions over time.

How long does the USE take?

The USE has 30 items and takes about 8 minutes. Typical use: Exploratory studies where you want a broad, diagnostic profile.

Can I calculate USE scores for many participants at once?

Yes. Collect the answers in Excel, Google Forms, Qualtrics or any other tool with one row per participant and columns Q1 to Q30, then upload the table as CSV or JSON on the "Score a dataset" tab. If participants filled in the USE here and sent you their downloads, drop all the PDF, CSV, JSON or ZIP files in at once instead. You get a score for every person, plus the mean, standard deviation and 95% confidence interval.

What is similar to the USE?

Similar questionnaires include System Usability Scale (SUS), Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), Computer System Usability Questionnaire (CSUQ).

Similar questionnaires

SUS

System Usability Scale Validated

The most widely used usability questionnaire. Ten alternating items, scored 0–100 and graded against a large normative database.

10 items 3 min 5-point scale
TAM

Technology Acceptance Model Validated

Davis’s Perceived Usefulness and Perceived Ease of Use scales, the core predictors of technology adoption.

12 items 4 min 7-point scale
CSUQ

Computer System Usability Questionnaire Validated

IBM’s 16-item questionnaire (version 3) for field and survey settings, with System Usefulness, Information Quality and Interface Quality subscales.

16 items 5 min 7-point scale
