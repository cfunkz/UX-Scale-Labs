Perceived usability

Post-study questionnaires for how usable a product feels overall.

SUS

System Usability Scale Validated

The most widely used usability questionnaire. Ten alternating items, scored 0–100 and graded against a large normative database.

10 items 3 min 5-point scale
UMUX

Usability Metric for User Experience Validated

Four items aligned with the ISO 9241-11 definition of usability. Designed to correlate closely with the SUS.

4 items 1 min 7-point scale
UMUX-LITE

UMUX-LITE Validated

Two positive UMUX items covering usefulness and ease of use. Includes the regression adjustment that aligns it with SUS.

2 items 30 sec 7-point scale
CSUQ

Computer System Usability Questionnaire Validated

IBM’s 16-item questionnaire (version 3) for field and survey settings, with System Usefulness, Information Quality and Interface Quality subscales.

16 items 5 min 7-point scale
PSSUQ

Post-Study System Usability Questionnaire Validated

The lab-study counterpart of CSUQ (version 3, 16 items). Comes with published norms for each subscale.

16 items 5 min 7-point scale

Task level

Short measures given straight after each task.

SEQ

Single Ease Question Validated

One 7-point question asked right after each task. Performs as well as longer post-task questionnaires.

1 item 12 sec 7-point scale
ASQ

After-Scenario Questionnaire Validated

Three items on ease, time and support information for a single task or scenario.

3 items 30 sec 7-point scale
NASA-TLX

NASA Task Load Index (Raw TLX) Validated

Six dimensions of perceived workload rated on 0–100 scales. Scored as Raw TLX, the unweighted mean.

6 items 2 min 0–100 rating

User experience & aesthetics

Broader experience: pragmatic and hedonic qualities, visual appeal.

UEQ

User Experience Questionnaire Validated

26 semantic-differential pairs across six scales, from Attractiveness to Novelty, with a public benchmark of 468 products.

26 items 4 min 7-point differential
UEQ-S

User Experience Questionnaire – Short Validated

Eight-item version of the UEQ that reports Pragmatic Quality, Hedonic Quality and an Overall value.

8 items 1 min 7-point differential
VisAWI-S

Visual Aesthetics of Websites Inventory – Short Validated

Four items measuring perceived visual aesthetics of a website or app screen.

4 items 30 sec 7-point scale
USE

USE Questionnaire Industry metric

Thirty items across Usefulness, Ease of Use, Ease of Learning and Satisfaction.

30 items 8 min 7-point scale

AI, chatbots & automation

Conversational interfaces and trust in automated systems.

CUQ

Chatbot Usability Questionnaire Validated

Sixteen items covering personality, onboarding, understanding, responses, error handling and ease of use for chatbots.

16 items 3 min 5-point scale
TiA

Trust in Automated Systems Validated

The Jian, Bisantz & Drury checklist: 12 items capturing trust and distrust in an automated or AI system.

12 items 2 min 7-point scale

Acceptance

Whether people will adopt and keep using a technology.

TAM

Technology Acceptance Model Validated

Davis’s Perceived Usefulness and Perceived Ease of Use scales, the core predictors of technology adoption.

12 items 4 min 7-point scale

Satisfaction & loyalty

Industry metrics used in product and CX tracking.

NPS

Net Promoter Score Industry metric

Likelihood to recommend on a 0–10 scale. NPS is the percentage of promoters minus the percentage of detractors.

1 item 12 sec 0–10 rating
CSAT

Customer Satisfaction Score Industry metric

A single five-point satisfaction rating. CSAT is reported as the share of satisfied (4–5) responses.

1 item 12 sec 5-point scale
CES

Customer Effort Score (CES 2.0) Industry metric

One agreement item on how easy it was to get something done. Effort predicts disloyalty better than delight.

1 item 12 sec 7-point scale

Quick checks

Handy for internal pulse checks. Not psychometrically validated.

BUX

Basic UX Pulse Not validated

Eight plain-language statements covering navigation, visuals, speed and control.

8 items 2 min 5-point scale
SRL

Self-Reported Learnability Not validated

Six statements about how easy a system was to pick up.

6 items 2 min 7-point scale

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a SUS score?

For each odd-numbered item subtract 1 from the answer, and for each even-numbered item subtract the answer from 5. Add the ten results and multiply by 2.5 to get a score from 0 to 100. The SUS calculator does this as soon as all ten items are answered.

What is a good SUS score?

The average SUS score is 68. On the Sauro–Lewis curved grading scale, 80.8 or more earns an A and anything below 51.7 is an F. Bangor et al. (2008) treat scores above 70 as acceptable.

Which UX questionnaire should I use?

It depends on when you ask. Use SEQ or NASA-TLX after each task, SUS or PSSUQ at the end of a usability test, UMUX-LITE for a short in-product survey and UEQ to measure the whole experience. The guide above covers more study types.

Can I score a whole study at once?

Yes, in two ways. If each participant filled in a questionnaire here and sent you their download, drop all the files in at once: PDF, CSV, JSON or the ZIP export, in any mix. Combine results scores every person and merges them into one dataset.

You can also collect answers in Excel, Google Forms, Qualtrics or any other tool, with one row per person and one column per item (Q1, Q2 and so on), and upload that table on the questionnaire’s Score a dataset tab whenever you are ready to analyse it. Both give the mean, a 95% confidence interval, reliability and a write-up.

Is it free, and where do my answers go?

Everything is free and needs no account. Scoring happens in your browser, so answers and uploaded files are never sent to a server. The privacy policy has the details.

How do I cite a questionnaire in my thesis or paper?

Each questionnaire’s Method tab lists the original references and gives BibTeX you can copy into LaTeX, Overleaf or Zotero. Always cite the original authors rather than this site.
