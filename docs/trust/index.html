When to use it

AI assistants, recommendation engines, autopilots, decision-support tools: anywhere over- or under-trust matters.

How it is scored

  1. Items 1–5 measure distrust and are reversed (8 − response) for the overall score.
  2. Overall trust is the mean of all 12 items after reversal (1–7, higher means more trust).
  3. Trust and distrust means are also reported separately, using raw responses.

Subscales

  • Trust (items 6–12) · items 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12
  • Distrust (items 1–5) · items 1, 2, 3, 4, 5

Reverse-scored items

1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Participants are not told which items are reversed.

Frequently asked questions about the TiA

How do I calculate the TiA score?

  1. Items 1–5 measure distrust and are reversed (8 − response) for the overall score.
  2. Overall trust is the mean of all 12 items after reversal (1–7, higher means more trust).
  3. Trust and distrust means are also reported separately, using raw responses.

This calculator applies these steps automatically as soon as every item is answered.

What is a good TiA score?

Higher means more trust on a 1–7 scale. For AI and automated systems the goal is usually calibrated trust, not the highest possible score.

How long does the TiA take?

The TiA has 12 items and takes about 2 minutes. Typical use: AI assistants, recommendation engines, autopilots, decision-support tools: anywhere over- or under-trust matters.

Can I calculate TiA scores for many participants at once?

Yes. Collect the answers in Excel, Google Forms, Qualtrics or any other tool with one row per participant and columns Q1 to Q12, then upload the table as CSV or JSON on the "Score a dataset" tab. If participants filled in the TiA here and sent you their downloads, drop all the PDF, CSV, JSON or ZIP files in at once instead. You get a score for every person, plus the mean, standard deviation and 95% confidence interval.
